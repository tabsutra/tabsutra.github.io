Predictions from your data, as it is

tabSutra builds foundation models for business data: time series, tables, event histories and relational data. One model forecasts what will happen, how likely it is, and how much it will be. It needs no feature engineering and no model built from scratch for each use case.

The data it works with

Time series

Demand, load or prices, alone or for thousands of related series together, with promotions, weather and holidays as inputs.

Tables

One row per customer, loan or transaction.

Event histories

Entities with any number of timestamped records. An example is a borrower with several accounts, each with 36 months of payment history.

Relational data

Several related tables, such as customers, orders and products, read as they stood at the time of each prediction.

The problem

Most prediction projects spend their time before any model is fitted.

  1. Data has to be joined and cleaned.
  2. Features have to be designed by hand: lags, rolling averages, summaries of past events.
  3. A model is then fitted and tuned for one question.
  4. A new question or a change in the data means doing much of it again.

Many firms have no team for this, so decisions run on rules of thumb and spreadsheets.

What tabSutra does

  • You pass your tables with their timestamps.
  • The model reads the past examples in that data and predicts for the new cases.
  • Results come in hours, not months.
  • With enough history, the model is fine-tuned on your data for higher accuracy.

Data that comes as sequences, such as the months of a loan account, is read as a sequence. A standard model can only see such data as separate rows or as hand-made summaries.

Predictions at two levels at once, such as each account and the customer, or each SKU and the store.

New products, new stores, new markets

  • A new product has no sales history. Neither does a new store or a new category.
  • Most forecasting methods need that history, so these cases are planned by hand.
  • tabSutra represents every product, store and customer as an embedding.

Forecasts from day one

A new product is forecast from the history of products like it.

Slow movers are not left out

An item with little history borrows from similar items with a lot of history.

Any number of items

The approach works the same for a hundred SKUs or ten million.

Expansion

A business entering a new region or category starts from what it already knows about similar regions and categories.

Use cases

Retail and consumer goods

Demand at SKU and store level, including new launches and new stores. Replenishment and promotion effects.

Lending and financial services

Credit scoring from account histories, early warning, collections prioritisation.

Energy and utilities

Load and generation forecasts, at any time granularity.

Electric vehicle fleets

Battery management systems report voltage, temperature, charge cycles and faults for every vehicle. tabSutra reads these histories across the whole fleet to predict downtime, battery health and residual value.

Manufacturing

Sensor monitoring, failure prediction, maintenance planning.

Built for production data

No look-ahead

A prediction uses only what was known at its time, so back-tests reflect real use.

Missing values are handled as missing

Nothing is filled in by guesswork.

Uneven timestamps are fine

Records can arrive at any time, and late arrivals are treated as late.

Consistent outputs

The same data gives the same prediction. Reordering columns changes nothing.

Ranges, not only point estimates

Every forecast comes with its uncertainty.

How it is used

  1. tabSutra is first run on your historical data and compared with your current method.

  2. It is then used through an API, or deployed on your own infrastructure so that data stays in-house.

  3. Either way, it can be fine-tuned on your full history.

Team

We are a team of applied ML scientists in India. We have worked on real-life data across industries, with methods ranging from classical machine learning to transformers.

The name

Sutra is Sanskrit for a thread, and for a short rule that holds a body of knowledge. tabSutra is one set of rules for structured data.
